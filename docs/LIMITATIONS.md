# Methodological & Ingestion Limitations

1. **Simple Graph Projection (Flattening):** While the raw SQLite database tracks separate layers (co-authorship, policy advisory, media narrative), the metrics and layout projection convert these relationships into a single weighted simple graph. This means co-authorship records heavily dominate topological centralities, while policy meetings and media mentions function as statistical noise.
2. **OpenAlex Citation Sampling Bias:** OpenAlex author bibliographies are capped at the top-20 most-cited publications. This introduces a structural bias towards older, highly cited research (such as historical Influenza or MERS studies) and under-represents acute, non-traditional, or highly disputed COVID-19 specific papers from 2020-2022.
3. **Unrecorded Stances (Missing Evidence):** Fauci's personal diary is a contemporary narrative and does *not* capture all statements or positions. For example, Marion Koopmans' stance on the Feb 1 conference call is unrecorded. Her algorithmic grouping in a specific community is a mathematical co-occurrence artifact and must not be interpreted as her holding a specific origin stance.
4. **Acronym Collisions in Grant Queries:** Acronym searches against NIH RePORTER (e.g. "VEO", "NCOH") match unrelated US grants using identical acronyms. Directly funded European consortium data is missing from NIH RePORTER.
5. **Static Temporal Aggregation:** The graph aggregates decades of academic co-authorship into a single static snapshot, creating a survivorship bias where past institutional collaborations appear contemporary to the 2020 origins discussion.
